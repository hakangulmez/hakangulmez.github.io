What it is

No standard dataset records which tasks a software product performs for its customers. The PCT dataset does, for every B2B software product that U.S.-listed software and IT-service firms describe in their annual reports.

It covers all 1,122 Item 1 business descriptions of fiscal years 2022–2025 and turns them into linked, structured records: from each filing to the offerings it names, to distinct economic products, to their customer-facing capabilities, to fixed product tasks with a completion condition. It is, in spirit, a task map for software products, comparable to occupational task databases for workers. Every record carries passage identifiers that link it back to the filing text.

How it is built

The dataset is produced by a fixed sequence of language-model instructions, one decision at a time. Each step has a defined unit, a defined input and a required output format. Every response passes deterministic validator checks and is archived together with its request, so each record can be traced and rerun.

Scored on top of the dataset Frontier replicability (R) rates each task against dated evidence of what frontier AI systems could do, and product AI functionality (T) records the depth of AI functionality the supplier discloses. At a glance

Consolidation separates what is sold as a distinct product from pricing tiers, payment methods, features, services and hardware. About two thirds of the named offerings become economic products of their own.

A worked example: HubSpot Service Hub, FY2022

HubSpot's annual report for the fiscal year ending 31 December 2022 names seven offerings. All seven are kept as separate economic products, none merged or excluded. Service Hub is classified as an embedded customer software module within the CRM Platform, and the filing states its purpose directly:

“Service Hub is our customer service software designed to help businesses manage, respond and connect with customers.”HubSpot, Form 10-K for FY2022, Item 1, passage P004

Records from the dataset

These seven source-linked records show the work behind portfolio accounting, workforce analysis, document workflows, integration, process automation, site planning, and data protection. Each retains the recorded task description, completion condition, product context, and capability roles. The R columns rescore the same FY2022 task at each frontier vintage; the examples include partial support, end-to-end coverage, and a task that remains at R0.

Reliability

A language-model measure must return the same answer to the same evidence before one can ask whether it is accurate. Each step was therefore rerun independently and compared unit by unit. The decisions that build the dataset are the most reproducible part of the measurement. The graded frontier judgment that produces R is less stable.

Reproducibility is not accuracy. The scores have not yet been benchmarked against independent expert coding; that benchmark is the next step.

Delivered as UTF-8 CSV files and an Excel workbook with a codebook. Keys link every level to its filing; array-valued passage references point to the Item 1 text. Blank cells mean missing, never zero, and UNKNOWN remains an explicit category.

Possible uses

  • Technology exposureMeasure how new technologies reach the tasks products perform, not only the tasks of occupations.
  • Task ownershipLink product tasks to occupational tasks to code whether work is done by a worker, a product or both.
  • Product strategyTrack how firms describe, bundle and extend their products over time.
