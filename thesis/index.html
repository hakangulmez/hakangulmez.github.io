Generative AI as a Task Shock: Product-Level LLM Substitution in B2B Software Markets

Hakan Zeki Gülmez · Master's thesis, TUM School of Management · Professorship of Applied Econometrics · Supervisor: Prof. Dr. Helmut Farbmacher · 2026

ContextQuestionMeasurementDesignFindingsInterpretationLimitsAccess

The findings at a glance

  • Technical supportability rose. The same baseline tasks became more supportable as the AI frontier expanded.
  • Revenue evidence is timing-sensitive. No same-period differential response is statistically distinguishable from zero. A negative lagged association weakens with firm-specific trends.
  • Product functionality changed. More exposed suppliers showed larger increases in the share of products with disclosed substantive AI functionality, conditional on baseline functionality and the other controls.

Abstract

Generative AI systems can increasingly perform work for which business customers currently purchase software. This thesis examines what this development has meant, to date, for the firms that supply such software. Using the business descriptions in 1,122 annual reports, I represent the B2B software products of U.S.-listed software and IT-service firms as sets of customer-facing tasks and score each task against dated evidence on the capabilities of frontier AI systems. Holding each firm’s 2022 tasks fixed, measured exposure more than triples by 2025, and most of the increase is already visible in 2023. Revenue has followed, if at all, only with a delay. A continuous-treatment difference-in-differences design detects no statistically distinguishable differential revenue response between more and less exposed suppliers in the same period. With a one-year lag, more exposed suppliers’ revenue grows more slowly, although the estimate halves once firm-specific trends are allowed. The clearest difference appears in suppliers’ products: conditional on initial functionality, more exposed suppliers add more substantive AI functionality. The structural coding replicates closely across independent runs; the graded exposure scores are noisier, and a stronger model rates them higher. Some disagreements involve AI agents that operate the incumbent’s software, which points to a question that the task framework leaves open: whether AI replaces the worker who uses the software or the software itself. The findings describe early adjustment, not the absence of later substitution.

Source: thesis abstract, reproduced verbatim.

Where it fits

The task-based approach treats work as a bundle of activities whose allocation between people and machines shifts with technology. Autor, Levy and Murnane (2003) show that computerization substituted for routine tasks while complementing other work. Acemoglu and Autor (2011) build the allocation of tasks between labor and capital into a broader framework, and Acemoglu and Restrepo (2019) add that technology can create new tasks as well as displace existing ones.

Measures of AI exposure follow the same logic on the worker side: occupational task descriptions, such as those in O*NET, are scored for what AI can do (Webb, 2020; Eloundou et al., 2024). Product-side work asks which offerings AI may displace (Green et al., 2026).

Task-based models treat capital as a single factor. Much of the work that occupational measures describe is done with software, and an AI gain can reach it by replacing the worker who operates the software or by replacing the software itself. Neither representation records the tasks a software product performs for its customers. This thesis builds that unit.

Source: thesis, Sections 2.1–2.3 and Discussion.

The question

Software products perform work that customers would otherwise have to do themselves. As external AI systems become more capable, they may support the tasks customers buy software to perform. The question is how this technical exposure is associated with early adjustment among software suppliers.

The contribution is a product-level task dataset built from annual reports with a validated language-model pipeline. It allows AI exposure to be measured for the tasks software products perform, tracked against dated capability evidence, and kept separate from suppliers' own AI functionality.

The thesis distinguishes three possible channels: AI may replace a worker directly, replace the worker who operates existing software, or replace the software itself. These are conceptual possibilities, not outcomes separately identified by the estimates. An agent operating a product still needs the product.

Crediting an agent that uses existing software need not mean its function can be supplied independently. This ambiguity motivates explicit task-ownership coding in future work.

How it is measured

The dataset starts with Item 1 business descriptions for FY2022–FY2025. It links disclosed products to their capabilities and then to the customer-facing tasks those capabilities support.

Source: thesis, Data and Measurement; counts cover the full product–task universe before the business-customer and financial screens.

The dataset is built by a fixed sequence of language-model instructions, each with a required output format and deterministic validator checks, and every response is archived with its request. The dataset page describes it in more detail.

The main exposure comparison uses a smaller, fixed basket: 3,251 numerically scored FY2022 tasks from 207 firms. Each firm's baseline tasks are rescored against successive frontier vintages. Later changes in product descriptions do not redefine that basket.

The external exposure score R measures technical supportability. A separate measure, T, records the depth of AI functionality the supplier discloses for its own products. Keeping these measures separate avoids building the supplier's response into exposure.

The dated frontier ledger

The ledger contains 66 dated operational capability records compiled from first-party documentation. It begins with the ChatGPT research preview of 30 November 2022; the latest records date from September 2026. Only records available by the relevant year-end enter each scored vintage.

Cumulative records at the year-end cutoffs are 1 / 16 / 33 / 56 for 2022 / 2023 / 2024 / 2025.

A task can be credited only with capabilities documented by that vintage; a citation to a later record is rejected. These checks constrain the supplied evidence dates, but do not eliminate every possible form of model look-ahead.

Measurement and reliability

The decisions that build the dataset are the most reproducible part of the measurement. In a fresh FY2022 run, about 92% of offering names recur, and structural decisions (consolidation, task surfaces, roles and market classification) agree in 86–99% of matched units. The graded frontier judgment is less stable.

The classifications use Gemini 2.5 Flash at temperature 0, with required formats and citation checks. On identical inputs, 95% of task-level R classifications in the repeatability sample are identical. Repeating the full scoring chain gives the same level for 62% of tasks and a level within one step for 87%.

A stronger model rates exposure higher. These are repeatability and model-comparison checks, not validation against an independent expert benchmark; that benchmark remains future work.

Source: thesis, Measurement reliability, the repeatability table and Table D1.

How firms are compared

A continuous-treatment difference-in-differences framework compares firms whose fixed FY2022 tasks gain more technical support with firms whose tasks gain less. Exposure varies continuously across suppliers. The main threshold is the GPT-4 announcement on 14 March 2023, applied to accounting period ends rather than filing dates.

The outcome is a chain-linked log revenue index. Each annual growth link uses current revenue and the prior-period comparative from the same filing, so both ends share a reporting presentation. Firm fixed effects absorb persistent level differences, and year effects absorb common annual movements.

Baseline exposure, size, growth, disclosed AI functionality and industry are interacted with year indicators. Contemporaneous functionality is excluded because it may itself be part of supplier adjustment. Panel uncertainty is clustered by firm.

  • Pooled benchmark: relates post-threshold revenue paths to the eventual 2022–2025 exposure change.
  • Contemporaneous dated dose: uses the latest year-end vintage available by each accounting period end.
  • Lagged dated dose: uses the preceding vintage, allowing an associated revenue pattern to appear later.

Fixing the basket prevents later disclosures from redefining exposure. It does not randomize product specialization or establish parallel revenue paths. The estimates remain conditional associations.

Source: thesis, Empirical Strategy and Interpretation and limits.

Revenue and products

Pooled benchmark

In the pooled model, a one-standard-deviation exposure difference is associated with approximately −1.2% revenue, with a 95% confidence interval from −4.9% to 2.5%. Neither this benchmark nor the contemporaneous dated-dose specification is statistically distinguishable from zero.

One-year-lagged exposure

In the lagged dated-dose model, a one-standard-deviation difference is associated with approximately −5.0% revenue (95% confidence interval −8.7% to −1.2%). The estimate halves once firm-specific trends are included. This specification and the pooled benchmark use different exposure definitions; their comparison is not a change in the lag alone.

Source: thesis, Revenue paths and the revenue-specification table. These are conditional associations.

Disclosed product functionality

More exposed suppliers raised their share of products with substantive AI functionality more, conditional on baseline functionality and the other controls. A one-standard-deviation exposure difference corresponds to approximately 4.5 percentage points. Without that conditioning, the association is much weaker.

Across pooled products in the estimation cohort, the share with T ≥ 2 rose from 16% to 28%. This weights firms by their number of products with known T; it is not the change for an average firm.

T2 requires a substantive customer-facing output, T3 connected workflow steps, T4 an operational action or persistent change, and T5 planning and execution across an end-to-end workflow. Suppliers already offering more AI functionality have less room to raise a bounded share. Baseline functionality accounts for much of the gap between controlled and uncontrolled estimates in the common-sample decomposition. This cannot separate economic adjustment from the mechanics of room to grow.

The positive pattern concerns substantive output features, rather than a general move toward deeper workflow integration. Because both exposure and functionality use the same classifier, their measurement errors may also be correlated. The finding describes a conditional pattern of disclosure changes, not a demonstrated protective mechanism.

Source: thesis, Supplier response and Discussion.

AI claims versus established functionality

After ChatGPT, annual reports picked up AI language quickly, and firms had reasons to overstate AI features. T is built so that this language alone does not move a product up the scale.

How T avoids counting AI talk

  • Tied to the product. The AI function must belong to this product or one of its named offerings. Firm-wide AI statements and AI that belongs to another product do not count.
  • Current, not planned. Roadmaps and future plans do not count.
  • Backed by the filing. Every level must be supported by cited Item 1 passages.
  • Words are not functions. Terms such as “AI-powered” or “agentic” do not establish autonomy, tool use or action on their own.
  • Checked twice. One step records whether a product currently offers AI. A second step scores its depth from the passages and can overrule the first.

Source: thesis, Product-level AI transition, Measurement reliability and Table B1. The instruction texts are not published.

Product-linked AI claims rose from 23% to 37% of products. Most are confirmed as substantive output, but roughly a quarter to a third of claims stop at assistance or are not confirmed. AI that carries out connected workflow steps or takes an action stays near 1–2% through FY2024 and reaches 5% in FY2025.

Why might adjustment take time?

The discussion considers explanations for the gap between technical supportability and revenue, without identifying their separate contributions.

Adaptation through existing products

An incumbent can incorporate the same external capability that makes its tasks easier to reproduce. Customer relationships, access to data and a place in established business processes may help it deliver that capability as a usable service. The disclosure results are consistent with adaptation, but do not show that it offsets substitution.

Switching and renewal

Customers may need to migrate data, redesign workflows and retrain users before changing products. Existing contracts and renewal cycles can add delay. The lagged pattern is consistent with this possibility, but the analysis does not observe contract duration or renewal decisions.

Competition through new suppliers

Substitution could arrive through AI-native suppliers packaging frontier capabilities for a particular workflow. The sample observes only listed incumbents. Because these entrants are mostly private and need time to win enterprise customers, their effect can also lag the frontier.

Source: thesis, Discussion. These are possible mechanisms, not separately established findings.

What this does and does not show

  • Identification remains uncertain. The pre-trend test has limited power, and further checks do not establish parallel trends.
  • The comparison is relative. It describes firms with larger versus smaller measured exposure changes; changes common to all suppliers fall outside it.
  • Technical support is not adoption. R records technical supportability; T records disclosed functionality, not verified customer use. The scores lack an independent expert benchmark.
  • The window and cohort are limited. The cohort requires reporting through FY2025. Firms that stop reporting fall outside this cohort, so the comparison may miss adjustment associated with exit. Results describe early adjustment among the observed suppliers, not the absence of later substitution.
What else was checked?

The pooled benchmark is re-estimated under alternative timing rules, sample boundaries, financial-data restrictions, task weights and outcome definitions. These checks address different vulnerabilities; the dated models are separate timing specifications rather than robustness checks of the same estimand.

Pre-period diagnostics have limited power. Profitability and mechanism outcomes show no statistically detectable relationship after multiple-testing adjustment and do not establish a route from exposure to performance. Identification and economic mechanisms remain uncertain.

Source: thesis, Robustness checks, Profitability and mechanisms, and Interpretation and limits.

Next steps

Extend the financial horizon, score post-2025 vintages on the same baseline tasks, and benchmark the scores against domain experts. Linking product tasks to O*NET would also allow explicit coding of task ownership: work performed by a worker, by a product, or jointly.
